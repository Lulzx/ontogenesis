# M17 — Invent an Euler Product in a Finite Toy World

## Required discovery

A globally defined sum over a finite arithmetic universe must be transformed
into a product over irreducible elements. Prime factorization cannot be given
as the desired representation.

## Status: reached — exact L3 finite Euler product

M7 discovers a common-divisor invariant, but the repository has no generic
finite arithmetic universe, irreducible-object discovery, multiplicative
function language, or product/sum equivalence prover. Earlier proof stages are
also unresolved.

M17 constructs that missing world. Universes are supplied extensionally as
sorted multisets; the learner infers irreducibles by checking whether a value
is a product of two present values, then searches a bounded univariate
arithmetic grammar for a local factor. It retains `1+r`: for every irreducible
`p`, subset products of `p` reproduce the squarefree universe exactly once, and
`prod_p (1+p)` equals the sum of all universe elements.

On three frozen unseen universes (32, 32, and 128 elements), the product
identity checks exactly and special-value operations fall from 217 to 59.
Missing-composite, non-squarefree, duplicate, and removed-element controls are
declined with baseline operation counts preserved. Prime lists, irreducible
labels, and Euler-factor templates are not supplied. M17 reaches
`L3_transferred_ontology_with_measured_utility` as a bounded finite integer
Euler-product schema; the extensional universe, grammar, and exact checker
remain supplied meta-ontology.

## Valid future result

The system must infer primitive factors from multiplication behavior, construct
local factors, verify that expanding their product accounts for every global
object exactly once, and demonstrate coefficient/special-value savings on
held-out finite universes. A supplied list of primes or prewritten Euler factor
template would not count as invention.
