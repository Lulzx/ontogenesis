# M16 — Invent a Toy Spectral Theorem

## Required discovery

From matrix dynamics, the system must identify an unlabelled structural
condition—symmetry—and connect it to real latent scales, orthogonal latent
directions, and spectral decomposition.

## Status: reached — exact L3 conditional routing

M9 invents scaled directions for one small hidden matrix and transfers the
relation to two transforms. It neither discovers matrix symmetry as the cause
nor proves spectral regularity for a family. Its integer bounded search cannot
handle arbitrary real symmetric matrices.

M16 repairs that gap in the toy integer domain. The system infers each hidden
matrix from one-step transitions, searches unlabelled scalar predicates over
the four inferred entries, and retains `a01-a10=0` as the first program whose
true/false split exactly matches the existence of a checked two-direction
certificate. For every admitted matrix, the checker verifies two orthogonal
exact latent directions, their power laws, and the rational spectral
decomposition `A = (s1/|d1|²)d1d1ᵀ + (s2/|d2|²)d2d2ᵀ`.

On nine unseen frozen tasks, five symmetric matrices (including a repeated
eigenvalue diagonal) are admitted and accelerated, with aggregate certificate
checks falling 109,039→83,182; four controls (complex-eigenvalue, defective,
repeated defective, and non-orthogonal diagonalizable) are declined with
identical exhaustive `no_solution` counts. Long-horizon operations fall from
180 to 59 per compatible task. False-positive routes and negative transfer are
both zero. M16 reaches `L3_transferred_ontology_with_measured_utility` while
matrix-entry addressability, the predicate grammar, transition probes, and the
spectral objective remain supplied. The result is a bounded exact integer toy
spectral theorem, not a general real spectral theorem.

## Completion criteria

The future system must contrast symmetric and nonsymmetric families, synthesize
candidate structural predicates without target labels, prove or checker-verify
their consequence in the toy domain, and show cheaper long-horizon dynamics.
Complex-eigenvalue, defective, and repeated-eigenvalue controls are required.
M9 is useful substrate, not a completed M16.
