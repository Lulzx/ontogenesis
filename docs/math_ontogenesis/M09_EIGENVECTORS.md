# M9 — Invent Eigenvectors

## Hidden dynamics

The matrix is not initially supplied. The system sees one-step vector
transitions generated by `[[2,1],[1,2]]`. It must invent latent objects that
make repeated application cheap without being given eigenvalue, eigenvector,
diagonalization, or spectrum.

## What it searched and discovered

It first enumerates small integer 2×2 transforms and finds the unique tested
matrix consistent with all transitions at discovery cost 1,945. It then
enumerates primitive integer directions and scale factors satisfying the raw
relation `A(v)=scale*v`. Two canonical directions survive:
`(1,-1)` with scale 1 and `(1,1)` with scale 3. These are eigenvectors in
effect, but their historical name was not used to generate them.

## Long-horizon and transfer evidence

For each direction, ten raw matrix applications exactly equal one scale power
times the original vector. Cost falls from 80 multiply/add units to four
direction/scale units, compression gain 76. The same relational search finds
two latent directions for an unseen diagonal transform and for a coordinate
swap. A 90-degree rotation control correctly has no real integer latent basis.

## Limits

Status is `bounded_verified`. Search is restricted to small integer matrices,
directions, and scales. It does not claim a general eigensolver, completeness
over real/complex vectors, or diagonalization of arbitrary matrices.
